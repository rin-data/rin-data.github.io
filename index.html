Applied EOQ modelling, quantity discount analysis, and transportation cost trade-offs (FCL vs. LCL) using a cut-and-try simulation across 32 order quantities to identify the landed-cost-minimising policy.

Tool

Key Finding — Optimal Policy
Order 400 units per requisition (3.75 orders/year) at $386/unit. Total landed cost = $637,401 — the lowest across all quantities tested. Basic EOQ alone (32 units) was far from optimal once transport economics, tiered pricing, and warehousing constraints were incorporated.
Case Questions Answered
Q1 — Was SIM at reorder point? Yes. Same-day free local delivery meant ROP = 0.
Q2 — EOQ without discount? EOQ = 32.4 units (D=1,500, S=$35, H=$100).

Key Finding
Two baristas is the optimal staffing level. Adding a second barista reduces daily cost by 93% ($1,644 → $121) and cuts average wait time from 24.9 to 4.3 minutes. A third barista saves only $34/day more while leaving staff idle 52% of the time — not worth the marginal gain.
Key Insight
In service systems, 100% utilization is never a goal — a single overloaded barista creates runaway queue costs that dwarf the labour saving. The moment a second barista is removed, waiting costs spike back up.

Two-part quality management case study. Part A uses X̄ and R control charts to monitor and evaluate cure time stability in a plastics manufacturing process. Part A — Key Finding
The baseline process (25 samples) appeared stable — all X̄ and R values fell within control limits. When 12 new production-run samples were plotted using the same limits, the process showed signs of being out of control: sample 6 (mean = 41.75) exceeded the UCL of 36.88, indicating a significant shift in the process mean and increasing variability.

Part B — Key Finding
With a mean thickness of 1.9625 mm and σ = 0.2096 mm, the current Cpk = 0.696 — well below the acceptable threshold of 1.0. Even centering the process at 1.9 mm only raises Cp to 0.795, still below 1.0. The process spread is too wide relative to specification width; variation must be reduced, not just the mean shifted.
Overall Learning
SPC and capability analysis are distinct but complementary tools. Control charts detect whether a process is stable over time; Cpk measures whether it meets specifications. A process can be in statistical control but still incapable — as demonstrated here, where the washer process is stable but too variable to reliably meet tolerance limits.
